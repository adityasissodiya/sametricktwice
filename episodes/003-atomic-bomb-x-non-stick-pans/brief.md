# 003-atomic-bomb-x-non-stick-pans · brief

**Pair:** A = Atomic-bomb project · B = Non-stick pans
**The bridge (one sentence, 30 words max: the one thing the viewer learns):** PTFE's
fluorine-rich surface resisted uranium hexafluoride in the Manhattan Project, then gave food
little to grip when the same plastic coated pans.
**A helped B by:** The Manhattan Project gave PTFE its first industrial job and helped scale a
material that later reached kitchens.
**B helped A by:** It did not; this history runs one way. The pan story instead reveals the
reverse engineering problem: making non-stick PTFE cling to aluminum.

## Myth check

- **The popular version of this story:** The Manhattan Project, the atomic bomb or NASA
  invented Teflon, and bombs themselves were coated with it.
- **What the sources actually support:** Roy Plunkett discovered PTFE at DuPont in 1938. Its
  first use was in the Manhattan Project's gaseous-diffusion work, where fluorine compounds
  demanded resistant materials. PTFE-coated cookware followed in the mid-1950s.
- **Words to avoid:** "invented for the bomb," "the bomb was coated," "NASA invented it,"
  and any claim that pans helped build the bomb. Say "atomic-bomb project" or "Manhattan
  Project equipment," not "inside the bomb."
- **Date caution:** The French patent record gives a December 1955 filing, while its patent
  family lists a December 1954 priority. The script says "mid-1950s" rather than forcing one
  date.
