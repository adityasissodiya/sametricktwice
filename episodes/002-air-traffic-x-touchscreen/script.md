## B1 · Hook
VO: Every tap on your phone uses an idea from a 1965 radar lab.
VIS: Cover frame: AIR TRAFFIC × TOUCHSCREEN over a phone whose screen is a radar scope, sweep already turning. A finger taps a blip; violet ripples; the blip is selected.
TXT: Air traffic × Touchscreen
NOTE: Enter on the first word; crisp and curious. Stress "every tap" and "1965".

## B2 · World A: a radar lab
VO: In 1965, Eric Johnson worked at a British radar lab. He wanted air traffic controllers to pick a flight on the screen with one touch.
VIS: Date badge 1965. A radar scope with a turning sweep and blips that move along their tracks. A finger hovers, then one blip gets a selection ring.
NOTE: Measured story pace. A small pocket of space after "In 1965" and around "Eric Johnson".

## B3 · The trick
VO: He put thin wires across the screen. Your finger changes how much charge a wire can hold, and a circuit feels it.
VIS: Front view of the radar screen with four blue wires and flight labels. A finger touches one wire; violet charge lines bend into the fingertip; a meter jumps for that wire; the wire and its label light up.
NOTE: Add momentum. Hit "changes" and "feels"; a tiny pause after the first sentence.

## B4 · Name the trick
VO: That is the trick: touch, charge, find.
VIS: Three cards: TOUCH (finger), CHARGE (charge lines), FIND (target). They shrink and rise.
TXT: Touch · Charge · Find
NOTE: Slow down. Separate "touch... charge... find" so each card lands with its word.

## B5 · World B: the keys disappear
VO: About forty years later, phone makers removed the keys. The whole front became a screen.
VIS: Date badge 2007. A phone with a small screen and a keypad; the keys fall away and the screen grows to fill the whole front.
NOTE: Reset, then lift on "removed the keys". Let "the whole front became a screen" land.

## B6 · Same trick, other world
VO: They used the same trick, with a grid of clear wires under the glass. Your finger takes charge where two wires cross, and the phone finds the spot.
VIS: Replay the B3 animation, re-skinned: phone glass instead of the radar screen, an orange grid of clear wires, the same finger path, charge lines and meter. The touched crossing lights up. The TOUCH · CHARGE · FIND badge lights up step by step.
NOTE: Let the recognition be audible on "the same trick". Even pace through "where two wires cross".

## B7 · Payback: touch returns to the tower
VO: Then touchscreens returned to air traffic control. Today, many British controllers move flight strips with a touch, not on paper.
VIS: The arrow reverses: orange flows back into blue. A paper flight strip with a pencil fades; electronic strips on a touchscreen take its place, and a finger drags one strip to a new column.
NOTE: A short pause before "Then" and a warm lift on "returned". Land "not on paper".

## B8 · Today
VO: Today, most phones and tablets feel your finger this way.
VIS: A grid of phones and tablets; touch ripples run across them one after another.
NOTE: Immediate and assured. Lean on "most".

## B9 · Kicker + map
VO: Air traffic control taught screens to feel a finger. Then touchscreens helped guide the planes. Same trick, two worlds.
VIS: Connection map: Air traffic (blue) and Touchscreen (orange) join with arrows both ways that merge into one blue-to-orange link.
