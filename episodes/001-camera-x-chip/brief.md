# 001-camera-x-chip · brief

**Pair:** A = Camera · B = Chip
**The bridge (one sentence):** Chips are printed with the same light-hardens-a-coating trick
that made the first photograph.
**A helped B by:** photographic chemistry (Kodak photoresist) and camera optics (an inverted
microscope, camera-store lenses, Nikon / Canon / Zeiss lithography optics).
**B helped A by:** image-sensor chips. The first portable digital camera (Kodak, 1975) saw
through a Fairchild chip.

## Myth check

- Lathrop and Nall did not coin the word "photolithography". Printers used it in the 1800s,
  so the script says "we still call it", not "they named it".
- Niépce's exposure time is not settled: the Ransom Center says several days, older books say
  8 hours. The script leaves it out.
- The date is given as 1826 or 1827 in different places. We follow the Ransom Center (1827)
  and say "around".
- "First digital camera" is too broad, because earlier electronic still cameras existed. Say
  "first portable" or "first handheld".

## Dialogue delivery plan

The approved VO never changes; each beat's `NOTE:` in script.md carries its direction.

- Hook emphasis and pace: crisp and curious; stress "200-year-old" and "every chip".
- Dates and names: a small pocket of space around "Around 1827", "1957" and "1975".
  Nicéphore Niépce = "nee-say-FOR NYEPS"; photolithography = "foh-toh-lih-THOG-ruh-fee".
- Mechanism rhythm: hit "made", "removed", "made" in B3; separate "coat... light... wash"
  in B4.
- Aha / reversal lift: audible recognition on "the same trick" (B6); a short pause and a warm
  lift on "Then chips helped cameras in return" (B7).
- Kicker landing: balance the two "chips" sentences, pause, then land "Same trick, two
  worlds." slower than the rest.

## Hooks

1. A 200-year-old camera trick makes every chip in your phone. (chosen)
2. Every chip in your phone is printed like a photograph.
3. The company that made your camera lens builds the machines that make your chips.
4. Your phone's camera and its processor are cousins.
5. The first chips were made with a microscope turned upside down.

## Spare facts

- In 1958 at Fairchild, Jay Last needed four matched lenses for the first step-and-repeat
  camera. He found them in a day at a camera store
  ([SPIE](https://spie.org/news/photonics-focus/mayjune-2021/jay-last-and-the-traitorous-eight)).
  Good for a follow-up Short, or as a B6 extra if a beat is cut.
- Kodak's KTFR resist (1961) became the standard for 1960s chipmaking
  ([SHMJ](https://www.shmj.or.jp/english/pdf/em/exhibi2420E.pdf)).
- Sasson's camera weighed 3.6 kg, used a lens from a Kodak movie camera, and took 23 seconds to
  write one 100 x 100 picture to cassette tape ([IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/first-digital-camera-history)).
- Around 1995, Nikon and Canon together held 70-80% of the chip-lithography market
  ([SHMJ](https://shmj.or.jp/innovation50/english/detail_T07E.htm)).
