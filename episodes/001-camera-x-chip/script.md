## B1 · Hook
VO: A 200-year-old camera trick makes every chip in your phone.
VIS: Cover frame: CAMERA × CHIP over a phone whose chip pulses. Circuit traces draw in, then a camera aperture snaps shut over the chip with a flash.

NOTE: Enter on the first word; crisp, curious and sure. Stress "200-year-old" and "every chip".

## B2 · World A: the first photograph
VO: Around 1827, Nicéphore Niépce put a thin layer of asphalt on a metal plate. He placed the plate in a camera at his window.
VIS: Date stamp c. 1827. A pewter plate gets a brown coat (small label: bitumen of Judea). A box camera at a window; rooftops project upside down onto the plate.
NOTE: Measured story pace. Give "Around 1827" and the name a small pocket of space: Nicéphore Niépce = "nee-say-FOR NYEPS".

## B3 · The trick
VO: Light made the asphalt hard. Lavender oil removed the soft parts. The hard parts made a picture.
VIS: Cross-section: gray plate, brown asphalt. Violet rays hit the bright areas and those patches lock hard. Oil flows over; soft patches dissolve; the pattern stays and becomes a framed picture.
NOTE: Add momentum. Hit the verbs "made", "removed", "made", with tiny pauses between the three sentences.

## B4 · Name the trick
VO: That is the trick: coat, light, wash.
VIS: The three steps shrink into three violet icons and park at the top of the screen as a badge.
TXT: Coat · Light · Wash
NOTE: Slow down. Separate "coat... light... wash" so each card lands with its word.

## B5 · World B: tiny transistors
VO: In 1957, two scientists at a U.S. Army lab needed tiny transistors. Jay Lathrop turned his microscope upside down, so the lens made patterns smaller.
VIS: Date stamp 1957. A transistor too big for its slot. A microscope flips 180 degrees; rays now converge, and a big pattern above becomes a tiny one below.
NOTE: Reset, then lift the energy on "tiny transistors". A small smile on "upside down"; let "smaller" land.

## B6 · Same trick, other world
VO: They used the same trick, with a coating from Kodak. We still call it photolithography: light, stone and writing.
VIS: Replay the B3 animation, re-skinned: germanium instead of the plate, orange Kodak coating instead of asphalt, a transistor pattern instead of rooftops. The badge lights up step by step. Then PHOTO · LITHO · GRAPHY splits into light, stone, writing.
NOTE: Let the recognition be audible on "the same trick". Say photolithography cleanly ("foh-toh-lih-THOG-ruh-fee"), then space "light, stone and writing" evenly.

## B7 · Payback: chips help cameras
VO: Then chips helped cameras in return. In 1975, a Kodak engineer built the first digital camera you could carry. Its eye was a chip.
VIS: The arrow reverses: orange flows back into blue. Date stamp 1975. A toaster-sized camera; inside it, a 100 × 100 grid chip fills pixel by pixel.
NOTE: A short pause before "Then" and a warm lift on "in return". Say "1975" clearly; land "Its eye was a chip."

## B8 · Today

## B9 · Kicker + map
